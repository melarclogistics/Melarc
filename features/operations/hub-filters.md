# Hub filters of the Ops Portal's reads

> **Status:** ACTIVE SPECIFICATION — implementation status is stated in section 16
> **Owns:** the optional `hub_id` filter of the four reads the hub switcher of the Ops Portal narrows: the list of pickup requests, the list of pickup runs, the message log and the queue summary
> **Slice:** none: a contract task of the Ops Portal redesign (task C3, the Product Owner's decision D12 of 6 November 2026), delivered between task P1.1 and the shell step R4

## 1. Purpose and scope

The hub switcher of the Ops Portal ([the Ops surface, section 3](../../surfaces/ops-portal.md#3-hub-visibility)) lets a person work one hub at a time, and a Platform Admin, whose grants reach every hub, browse "All hubs". Until this task only the built lists of staff, riders and vendors took a hub, so the pickup queues, the runs, the messages and the counts beside the menu could not follow the switcher: they showed every hub the session's grant reaches. This feature gives the four reads that make up those screens an **optional, additive `hub_id`**, in the one convention the three lists already follow, and owns what it means for each of them.

**In scope:** the parameter `hub_id` on `listPickupRequests`, `listPickupManifests`, `listNotifications` (the message log) and `getQueueSummary`; what it does inside the scope the read already has; what it answers for a hub the session's grant does not reach and for a hub that does not exist; how a count and its list keep agreeing when it is given; what it does to the audit; what it does for the Vendor and the Rider who share two of the reads; and what the three older lists that already took a `hub_id` answer for a hub outside a staff session's grant (task F: the same as the four reads, without a statement).

**Out of scope:** the switcher itself and the way the Portal draws the answers (step R4: [the Ops surface](../../surfaces/ops-portal.md#4-navigation-model)); the lists that already take a hub (`listStaffIdentities`, `listRiders`, `listVendorAccounts`), whose `hub_id` keeps the meaning it had and which are changed in one thing only, their answer for a hub outside a staff session's grant ([section 5](#5-behavior), `AC-HUBF-13`); the reads of one record by its identifier, whose `HUB_SCOPE_VIOLATION` is the scope probe's and does not change; `lookupRecords` ([global lookup](global-lookup.md): a search finds a record wherever the session may open it, and a code typed from a paper slip may belong to another hub); the other lists of built operations that take no hub (run custody handovers, sessions: the switcher does not narrow their screens); a filter by more than one hub; a breakdown of a count by hub; any change of a key, a scope, a state, an error code or an audit rule.

## 3. Actors and surfaces

A signed-in **Ops Portal staff session** of any bundle that holds the key of the read. `getQueueSummary` and `listNotifications` are the Ops Portal's alone: a Vendor session is `PERMISSION_DENIED` and a Rider's bearer credential, which these operations do not take, is `SESSION_INVALID`. `listPickupRequests` also serves a Vendor (its own organisation's requests), and `listPickupManifests` a Rider (the runs assigned to it and dispatched): for them the filter narrows inside what they are shown. **A Rider is not served `listPickupRequests` or `getPickupRequest` at all** (task A3.1 of the audit follow-up, the Product Owner's decision of 7 November 2026, contract 5.99.0: [section 5](#5-behavior)). The only code that sends the filter is the Ops Portal's, from step R4.

## 4. Preconditions

The key of the read: `pickup.read` for the requests and the runs, `notification.read` for the messages. The summary is `self`: no key admits the call, any signed-in Ops Portal staff session is answered, each queue by the key of its own list (`pickup.read`, `staff.read`) and, for the two approver's queues, the decision key as well, and a session that holds none of them gets an empty list ([AC-QSUM-01](../reporting/reporting-data-dictionary.md#13-acceptance-criteria)). A session that is refused a read is refused as it is without a `hub_id`.

## 5. Behavior

**The parameter.** `hub_id` is an optional query parameter, `{ type: string, format: uuid }`, on the four reads: the same parameter and type as on the three lists that already take a hub (each of the four carries a description of what it means for that read, which those three do not), and, on the summary, the `400 VALIDATION_FAILED` that those lists already declare. It is one value or none. A value that is not a UUID, an empty one, one written with spaces around it or as `urn:uuid:...`, and one given twice are `VALIDATION_FAILED` naming `hub_id`, as they are on those lists; a UUID in capitals is the same hub as in lower case.

**What it means.** It is **a filter that can narrow what the caller may see and never widens it: it is authorization-checked and never trusted.** It adds one condition to the statement the read already runs, *the hub of the record is the one named*, and the scope is still decided where it always was: by the row-level security of the transaction the read runs in, under the grant of the read's own key (the hubs of the grant, or every hub where the grant is of all hubs), which nothing in the request can change. The hub of a record is the column that already scopes it: `responsible_hub_id` for a pickup request, a pickup run and a message, `primary_hub_id` for a staff profile. A message's hub is the hub the record answered to **when the act was done**: it is copied from the act's envelope and never changes, so a message about a record that later moves hub stays under the hub it was raised in.

**One function for the filter.** The list and the count of a queue call one function for what they contain (`pickupRequestCriteria`, `pickupManifestCriteria`, `staffIdentityCriteria`), and the hub is one more of its conditions, so a queue's count with a hub is the number of records its list returns with that hub **by construction**. The hub never enters the security context (it is a parameter of the statement, never of the transaction: a context built from it would be a scope, and a scope comes from the grant alone).

**A hub the session's grant does not reach, and a hub that does not exist.** Both are **an empty page** (status 200, no items, no next page token) on a list, and, on the summary, **every entry the session may read present with `count` 0 and no `oldest_at`**: never `HUB_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, never `NOT_FOUND`, never a refusal of any kind, never an absent entry. Row-level security has removed the rows, the filter has nothing left to keep, and no code asks whether the hub exists, because the two answers must be one: a caller learns nothing from the answer of whether a hub exists or of what it holds beyond its own grant, and nothing from the time the answer takes (the next paragraph). This is the convention of the staff, rider and vendor lists, and it is not that of the creation of a request, which names its hub in its body and is told informatively (`AC-SLICE-001-82`). **The read of one record by its identifier does not change**: a staff member's read of a record of a hub outside their grant is still `HUB_SCOPE_VIOLATION` by the scope probe ([SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) section 14.4b), and an identifier that matches nothing is still `NOT_FOUND`.

**No statement is run for a staff hub outside the grant: the time is the same too (task A1, the Product Owner's audit of 7 November 2026).** The hub filter is a leakproof equality, so the database evaluates it as an index condition *before* row-level security discards what the session may not read: for a hub outside the grant it walked that hub's index (0.7 to 0.9 seconds at 200,000 rows, [section 14.1](#141-measured-at-400000-requests)) and removed every row, while an identifier that names no hub found nothing and answered in microseconds. Seeded hub ids are guessable, so a staff session could learn that a hub exists and roughly how much it holds. For a **staff session whose grant of the read's key is a set of hubs**, a `hub_id` that is not in the set is therefore answered **before a transaction is opened**: the empty page of a list, and on the summary every entry the session may read at `count` 0 with no `oldest_at`, decided **key by key** (a queue is counted in the grant of its own list key, so a key whose grant holds the hub is still counted, and so is a grant of every hub, whatever hub it names; a hub inside the grant, or no `hub_id`, is read as it always was). A hub that exists outside the grant and a hub that does not exist take the same path and so the same time, which is the time of the request's own guards. The answer is the one row-level security gives: the set compared is the very set the transaction would have run under (`authorized.scope`, which `contextOfAuthorization` writes to the transaction), compared as lower-case identifiers, so a UUID in capitals is the same hub and nothing about the answer, its status, body and headers, changes. The value is still judged first (a `hub_id` that is not a UUID is `VALIDATION_FAILED` before anything else), and the audit is unchanged (a page with no row writes none). It is a second place where the grant is read beside the row-level security, which is why it compares the same set and why `hub-filters.db.test.ts` pairs it with a trace of the statements the application's login sent.

**`listAccountingExports`, when it is built, answers the same way** (the audit follow-up of 7 November 2026, task A3.8; [accounting export](../payments/accounting-export.md) section 5 item 5 holds it too): its `hub_id` (the exports of one hub; an `ALL_HUBS` record carries none) is judged as a UUID and, for a staff session whose grant of `payment.ledger.read` is a set of hubs that does not hold it, is the empty page with no transaction and no statement, by the same function (`hubOutsideStaffGrant`).

**The three older lists answer the same way (task F, the Product Owner's decision of 7 November 2026, afternoon: every hub filter behaves the same; `AC-HUBF-13`).** `listStaffIdentities` (`staff.read`; the filter reads the profile's primary hub), `listRiders` (`dispatch.read`; the rider's primary hub) and `listVendorAccounts` (`vendor.read`; the responsible hub of the account's organisation) already took a `hub_id` in the convention this feature follows (an empty page for a hub outside the grant: [AC-SLICE-000-102](../identity/vendor-authentication.md) and the lists' own route tests), and for a hub the grant does not reach they walked the same kind of index, over tables of staff, riders and vendors that are small beside the request and message tables. They are given the shortcut of the four reads so that no read that takes a `hub_id` behaves another way: for a staff session whose grant of the list's key is a set of hubs, a `hub_id` that is not in it is an empty page **before a transaction is opened** (the same function, `hubOutsideStaffGrant`, over the same `authorized.scope`), a hub inside the grant, a grant of every hub and no `hub_id` are read as they were, and a value that is not a UUID is `VALIDATION_FAILED` naming `hub_id` before anything else (the three services now judge it themselves, as the four reads do; the contract's validation answered it first, on the route, so no client sees a difference). One boundary, **closed by task A3.5** (the audit follow-up, `AC-HUBF-14`): the table of staff profiles also admits a profile's own row to its holder whatever the grant, so a staff session whose grant of `staff.read` reaches no hub, and which names its own primary hub, is shown its own profile for that hub, as it is without the filter: a filter only ever narrows what the same session sees without it. The own hub is told from the session (`hubOfTheSession`: the hubs the session was issued with, a staff member's primary hub; no statement), so every other hub, and a hub that does not exist, is still the short answer. No seeded bundle holds `staff.read` at no hub, so the case does not arise from a real grant; it is held by tests all the same. A Vendor and a Rider reach none of the three lists (a Vendor is refused, a Rider's bearer credential is not one the lists take), so there is no ownership to name in their statements.

**The queue summary.** `getQueueSummary` takes the same optional `hub_id` and runs each count **in the context of the grant of that queue's own list key**, as it does without it, with the hub as a condition of the count's statement (a key whose grant is a set of hubs that does not hold the hub is not counted at all: its queues are `0`, as above). Which entries are present does not depend on the hub: a queue the session may not read is absent for every hub, an approver's queue is absent without its decision key whatever the hub, and no entry the session may read is ever absent because of the hub (an absent entry means "not yours", and a `0` means "read, and nothing waits", for any hub). An entry is `queue`, `count` and, when the count is not 0, `oldest_at`, which is the earliest moment among **that hub's** records of the queue; the answer names no hub and holds nothing more ([AC-QSUM-05](../reporting/reporting-data-dictionary.md#13-acceptance-criteria)). Everything else about the read is as the data dictionary says and the filter does not change it (each key's counts are one snapshot, each count is bounded by `AC-QSUM-13`, the answer is `no-store`, the read writes no audit event and is `background` activity). **The staff approver's queue counts profiles by their primary hub**, as `listStaffIdentities` does with `hub_id`. A profile with no primary hub (the seeded Platform Admin has none, and so may any profile made without one) is therefore in the count without `hub_id` for a session whose `staff.read` grant is of every hub (a Senior Ops user, whose grant is its own hubs', sees it in no count) and in the count of **no** hub. The five pickup queues have a hub on every record. **Nobody adds the hubs' counts up to the all-hub count, neither the server nor a client**: for the pickup queues the sum happens to equal it, for the staff queue it does not.

**Combination and paging.** `hub_id` combines with every other filter of the read as "and" (`state`, `service_date`, `manifested`, `one_package_exception_state` and `cutoff_exception_state` on the requests; `state` and `service_date` on the runs; `state`, `audience`, `channel` and `subject_id` on the messages). A list pages as it did: a page token is only a position in the list's order and is used with the filters it was given, so a client that changes the hub starts again at the first page. A list carries no `ETag`, and the summary none.

**Audit.** The audit of a read does not change with the filter. A list writes one `auth.hub_boundary.crossed` for **each hub other than the actor's primary hub that the rows of the page it returns represent**, naming the hub and the operation and carrying no row ([audit.md](../../contracts/audit.md) section 5.4): a Platform Admin whose primary hub is the first, asking for the second, writes one for the second; asking for the first, none; a Platform Admin with no primary hub has no hub of its own, so the page of whichever hub it names writes one for that hub; an officer, who reaches its own hub alone, none. **A page with no row writes none, whatever hub was named**: a filter that matched nothing is not an attempt on a hub. The summary writes none, with or without a hub, because it returns no record.

**The Vendor and the Rider.** The two reads they share are narrowed by the filter inside what row-level security shows them, and no further. A Vendor's list of requests is its own organisation's, across hubs; with `hub_id` it is those of that hub, never another vendor's and never another hub's (`responsible_hub_id` is in every representation of a request already, so the filter shows nothing new; today a request is made in its organisation's hub, `AC-SLICE-001-82`, and the reassignment of a request's hub is not built, so a Vendor has requests in two hubs only where the organisation has them, and the tests arrange them). A Rider is not served the list of requests (task A3.1), and its list of runs is the runs assigned to it and dispatched, with `hub_id` those of that hub. Neither crosses a hub, so neither writes an audit event.

**Their statement says what they own, so that the hub's size is not in the time (task A1).** A Vendor and a Rider hold no grant of hubs, so the shortcut above is not theirs: their rows are scoped by what they own, and the `hub_id` of a hub that exists but holds none of their records used to take the time of the hub (0.7 seconds for a hub of 200,000 requests, 75 milliseconds for 20,000 runs, end to end: [section 14.2](#142-measured-after-task-a1-the-time-of-a-hub-outside-the-grant)). Their policy is one `CASE` expression, which the planner cannot use as an index condition, so a statement that names a hub read the hub's rows through the hub's index and let the policy discard every one that was not theirs. The statement now names the ownership as the policy tests it, as a plain condition: a Vendor's list of requests `vendor_organization_id = melarc.current_vendor_organization_id()` and a Rider's list of runs `assigned_rider_id = melarc.current_rider_id()` (the context's own functions, the ones the policies call; nothing of the request is in them), and the planner starts from the owner's index (`pickup_request_vendor_idx`, `pickup_manifest_rider_idx`) with the hub as a filter on the owner's own rows. The condition only restates the policy, which still decides: it can narrow and never widen, and the rows and their order are the ones they were. The time then depends on what the caller owns and not on what the hub holds. In the database, with 200,000 requests in the second hub and 20,000 runs (`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)`, the best of four): a Vendor with 500 requests takes 0.15 milliseconds for that hub (it took 423) against 0.07 for a hub that does not exist, a Vendor with 10,000 takes 2.6 against 0.07, and a Rider with 40 runs takes 0.06 (it took 50) against 0.05. A Vendor or a Rider with a very large history of its own still reads that history for a hub that exists and not for one that does not, a difference that is its own volume and nothing of the hub's ([section 14.2](#142-measured-after-task-a1-the-time-of-a-hub-outside-the-grant)). **A Rider is not served the list of requests at all** (task A3.1 of the audit follow-up, the Product Owner's decision of 7 November 2026: option (iii) of [section 14.3](#143-the-riders-list-of-requests), contract 5.99.0, after the Product Owner's rule of the afternoon that ignored a Rider's `hub_id` for want of an owned predicate): `listPickupRequests` and `getPickupRequest` name the browser session alone, so a Rider's bearer credential is `SESSION_INVALID`, as on every staff read, and there is no statement to time and no hub to judge or to ignore.

**What does not apply.** The filter is a read: it has no cancellation, no retry of its own, no idempotency key and no terminal state, and nothing is kept of it; a request is answered or refused, and asking again reads again. A count and a list are two reads at two moments (each key's counts of the summary are one snapshot, a list is another), so a count equals its list for the same records, and a record made between the two reads is in the later one only.

**What does not change.** Without `hub_id` every read answers exactly as it did. No key, state, error code, setting or audit event is added; the scope of a read is the grant of its key and not a request's say; the staff, rider and vendor lists read as they did for every hub inside the grant, for a grant of every hub and with no `hub_id`, and answer a hub outside a staff session's grant as they did (an empty page), now without a statement (task F, `AC-HUBF-13`).

No permission is added and none admits the filter: each read keeps its own key and scope. [permissions.md](../../contracts/permissions.md); [data-scope-registry.md](../../contracts/data-scope-registry.md) section 7.2.

**The shortcut and the policy agree, by construction (task A1):** for a staff session the answer for a hub outside the set of its grant is given without a statement ([section 5](#5-behavior)), and it is the answer row-level security would have given, because the hubs compared are the hubs the transaction would run under; an all-hub grant is never answered that way (it reaches every hub), and a Vendor and a Rider are scoped by what they own, so it is not theirs. **Negative access:** a hub outside the grant is an empty page and a `0`, never a refusal and never a row; an all-hub grant held beside another key does not widen the key being exercised (`GBT-GS1`); a session whose grant reaches no hub reads nothing for any hub; on the three lists, a request with no session is `SESSION_INVALID` and a session without the key `PERMISSION_DENIED`, each before the value of `hub_id` is judged, so that a caller who may not use a read learns nothing of its schema; the summary, which no key admits, judges the value for any signed-in Ops Portal staff session ([section 5](#5-behavior)).

## 13. Acceptance criteria

### `AC-HUBF-01` — The list of pickup requests narrows to one hub, and only narrows

```text
Given pickup requests of a first hub and of a second hub in several states, and sessions of an Ops Staff officer and a Senior Ops user of the first hub, a Platform Admin with no primary hub and a Platform Admin whose primary hub is the first,
When each asks listPickupRequests with hub_id of the first hub, of the second hub and with none, alone and together with state, service_date, manifested, one_package_exception_state or cutoff_exception_state,
Then the list with a hub holds exactly the requests of that hub that the same session and the same other filters are given without it, in the same order, read to its last page,
And for the Platform Admin the lists of the two hubs together are the whole of the list without hub_id, with no request in both,
And the officer and the Senior Ops user, naming their own hub, are given exactly the pages they are given without hub_id,
And a page of a list with hub_id continues, with the next page token and the same hub_id, to the end of that list, each request once.
```

**Governs:** this feature, section 5 · **Surface:** Melarc Ops · **Test level:** API · **Code:** none

### `AC-HUBF-02` — The list of pickup runs narrows to one hub, and only narrows

```text
Given pickup runs of a first hub and of a second hub in several states and on several days, and the four sessions of AC-HUBF-01,
When each asks listPickupManifests with hub_id of the first hub, of the second hub and with none, alone and together with state or service_date,
Then the list with a hub holds exactly the runs of that hub that the same session and the same other filters are given without it, in the same order, read to its last page,
And for the Platform Admin the lists of the two hubs together are the whole of the list without hub_id, with no run in both,
And a list carries neither the stops nor an ETag, as without the filter.
```

**Governs:** this feature, section 5 · **Surface:** Melarc Ops · **Test level:** API · **Code:** none

### `AC-HUBF-03` — The message log narrows to one hub

```text
Given messages of a first hub and of a second hub in several states, audiences and channels, and the four sessions of AC-HUBF-01,
When each asks listNotifications with hub_id of the first hub, of the second hub and with none, alone and together with state, audience, channel or subject_id,
Then the list with a hub holds exactly the messages of that hub that the same session and the same other filters are given without it, newest first, read to its last page,
And a message is in the hub the record it is about answered to when the act was done, whatever hub the record is in now,
And a subject_id of a record of the second hub with hub_id of the first is an empty page (the two filters are "and", and the page says nothing of where the subject is),
```

## 14. Unresolved requirements

The decision D12 of 6 November 2026 did not settle the points below. The Product Owner decided the first two the same evening, and the third, the fourth and the fifth in the audit of 7 November 2026. The sixth was built the **conservative** way (the one that shows no more than the read already shows and changes nothing already built), is recorded as a finding for the Product Owner, and costs what it says to change. It does not block the task.

1. **Decided (Product Owner, 6 November 2026): a Vendor and a Rider may send `hub_id` to the two reads they share** (`listPickupRequests`, `listPickupManifests`), and it only narrows what they already see: nothing new is shown (`responsible_hub_id` is in every representation they already read) and no audit event is written for them. The alternative, to refuse a non-staff caller with `PERMISSION_DENIED`, is not taken.
2. **Decided (Product Owner, 6 November 2026): a staff profile with no primary hub appears only in the count of a session whose `staff.read` grant is of every hub, when no hub is chosen, and never in a single hub's.** The count with a hub is by primary hub, as the staff list's own `hub_id` is, so the per-hub counts of the approver's queue are not expected to add up to the all-hub count: that is intended. The rule is owned by [the data dictionary, section 5.5.6](../reporting/reporting-data-dictionary.md#556-queuestaffawaitingapproval--staff-profiles-waiting-for-approval), where the queue is defined, and is written there. The Product Owner also asked for it in [the Ops surface, section 4](../../surfaces/ops-portal.md#4-navigation-model), beside the menu counts: step R4, which builds those counts, writes it there.
3. **Decided (Product Owner, 7 November 2026): a message keeps the hub it was sent under and does not follow its record.** The hub of a message is the hub the record answered to when the act that raised it was done: it is copied from the act's envelope and never changes, so a message about a record that later changes hub stays under the hub it was raised in, in the hub filter of `listNotifications` and in the message log. This is what is built (the reassignment of a request's hub is not built, so the case has not yet arisen); the task that builds the reassignment does not move messages, and it needs no rule for them and no change to the guards of the notification table. The alternative, a message that answers to its record's current hub, is not taken.
4. **Decided (Product Owner, 7 November 2026): the other built reads that take no hub get `hub_id` only when a screen needs it (step R7).** `lookupRecords` (a search finds a record wherever the session may open it, and a code typed from a paper slip may belong to another hub), `listRunCustodyHandovers`, `listSessions` and the like are outside D12's four reads, so the switcher does not narrow those screens and none is given the parameter now. A read that is given one when its screen needs it follows this feature's convention, including the answer for a hub outside the grant and, for a staff session, the answer without a statement ([section 5](#5-behavior)); it is added in the contract task of the step whose screen needs it, with its own criteria and measurement.
5. **Decided (Product Owner, 7 November 2026, the audit's finding A1): the time a read takes must not tell a caller whether a hub outside its grant exists, or how large it is.** It did, and for the Vendor and the Rider as much as for staff: a hub that holds 200,000 rows took 0.7 to 0.9 seconds for a staff session of another hub (and 0.7 seconds for a Vendor of the first hub, 0.23 seconds for a Rider's list of requests, 75 milliseconds for a Rider's 20,000 runs, end to end: [section 14.2](#142-measured-after-task-a1-the-time-of-a-hub-outside-the-grant)), while an identifier that names no hub finds nothing in the index and took milliseconds ([section 14.1](#141-measured-at-400000-requests)); seeded hub ids are guessable. *Built (task A1):* for a staff session whose grant of the read's key is a set of hubs, a `hub_id` outside it is answered with no transaction and no statement, in the four reads (key by key on the summary), so that a hub that exists and a hub that does not take the same, short time (`AC-HUBF-05`, [section 5](#5-behavior)); a Vendor's statement names its organisation and a Rider's list of runs its assignment, so that the planner starts from their own rows and the hub's size is not in the time; a Rider's list of requests, which is empty for every hub, judges the hub and does not apply it (`AC-HUBF-12`: the Product Owner's fallback, for the one read where no ownership can be named). ### 14.1 Measured at 400,000 requests

Measured on 6 November 2026 on PostgreSQL 18.6 with the runtime role and the real security context (the policies of the tables, the grant of one hub or of every hub), for the statements the reads run: **400,000 pickup requests** in two hubs of 200,000 (about 180,000 confirmed in each hub, the rest cancelled or declined; 400 pending, 400 requested exceptions and 514 late bookings, all in the first hub), 5,000 approved exceptions, **400,000 messages** (200,000 in each hub) and **30,000 runs**, with no stop on any run.

**How to read the figures.** Each is the execution time that `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` reports, the best of three after a warm-up (planning, 0.2 to 18 milliseconds, is not in it), in milliseconds, for a session of **one hub** (an Ops Staff or Senior Ops user) and for a **Platform Admin** (who reads both hubs). The list statements are rebuilt in the test from the criteria functions and the column lists the services use (the run list was measured selecting only the identifier, where the service selects the thirteen columns of a run: that difference was not measured). Because the pending requests, the requested exceptions and the late bookings are all in the first hub, the small counts of those three queues for the second hub measure an empty index range and not the policy removing rows (the runs in progress are in both hubs, 300 in each). The figures give the order of magnitude and the shape of each plan; a plan at the production volumes is what to read before relying on them.

`uuid_eq` is leakproof on this server (`pg_proc.proleakproof`), so `responsible_hub_id = $1::uuid` is evaluated as an **index condition before the row-level-security filter**: that is why most of the reads below get faster with a hub. **No index and no migration were added.**

The index condition finds the other hub’s rows and the policy then removes every one, so the read costs about what the other hub’s rows cost (0.7 to 0.9 seconds for the lists and the pool count at 200,000 rows a hub) and never more than the same session’s read without a hub; an identifier that names no hub finds nothing in the index (0.06 milliseconds when it was measured for task A1). That was **a leak of the hub’s existence and size through the time** (section 14, item 5): a staff session whose grant is a set of hubs is no longer run these statements for a hub outside it, so the one-hub figures of the last column are what the database would charge for statements that task A1 stopped asking; the Platform Admin’s figures of that column (a hub inside its grant) are unchanged. The switcher (step R4) offers only the hubs the session lists, so this is the case of a selection that went stale, or of a caller that is not the Portal.

**The request list’s order is not served by the hub’s index.** The list is ordered by `created_at, id`; with a hub the statement reads the hub’s rows through `pickup_request_hub_idx` and sorts them (a first page in 0.8 seconds for a session of one hub, against 1.4 seconds without a hub, which scans the table), so the filter is not the cost. An index on `(responsible_hub_id, created_at, id)` is the candidate for the read with a hub, and one on `(created_at, id)` for the read without; both are predictions and neither was measured. It is recorded in [the implementation plan](../../delivery/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#3a-the-ops-portal-redesign) beside the other performance tasks. The message log’s index (`notification_hub_recent_idx`) does serve its order.

**The approver’s queue was unstable with a hub, and its condition was changed.** With the hub as a condition of the statement, the count of the requests waiting for an exception’s decision took **404 ms** in the first run (30 ms without a hub), **1,277 ms** (Platform Admin 891 ms; 36 ms without a hub) in the second and 635 ms (Platform Admin 469 ms; 25 ms without a hub) on a third data set with 100,000 approved exceptions beside the others; the three come from one generator and are not the same rows. The plan showed why: the hub’s 400 pending requests were estimated at one row, so the planner ran the condition that names the requests which can show an exception in the state (`id in (subquery)`) as a **nested loop semi join that reads the 400 requested exceptions again for each of them** (`loops=400`, the exception policy 80,200 times), where without a hub it hashes the set once and joins by key. Whether it does depends on the statistics and not on the query: with 1,600 pending requests in the first hub the same statement took 264 ms, and with 400 pending requests in each hub 23 ms. A plan that is 13 to 35 times slower than the same statement without a hub on some data, and not on other data, is the instability; by its shape (the inner set read once for each outer row) its cost grows with the product of the two sizes, which the plan showed and the runs did not measure as a function of the size.

The condition is now `id = any (array(subquery))`, **read once whatever the planner estimates**: with a hub 39 ms (Platform Admin 27) on the second data set, 23 ms (19) on the third, 104 ms (60) with 1,600 pending requests and 22 ms (14) with pending requests in both hubs; without a hub 34 ms (27), 33 ms (27), 209 ms (58) and 21 ms (24). A fence (`offset 0`) did not help (1,063 ms). The meaning is the same: the old form and the new returned the same count, or a list of the same length, in every case measured, and `pickup-request-criteria.db.test.ts` compares the new form with the old one, kept in the test as text, the contents of the lists included, across the five scopes, the three hubs, the three states of an exception and 48 combinations of the other filters (the Product Owner’s condition for accepting the change).

**The new form is not faster in every cell.** Of 60 pairs measured across the four data sets (the count and the list, with and without a hub, in both scopes, for the requested exceptions in all four and for the approved exceptions in the last) it was faster or equal in 45 and slower in 15. In 13 of the 15 the difference is under 30 milliseconds; the two larger are the count of the requested exceptions with no hub for a session of one hub with 1,600 pending requests (155 ms to 209 ms) and the count of the approved exceptions with no hub for a session of one hub with 100,000 approved (4.5 to 6.2 seconds). The approver’s queue is read with a hub by every session that chose one hub and without a hub by every session that did not, and both are now steady.

**The approved and the refused exceptions.** The same function serves `one_package_exception_state=APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, which no screen asks for and which are history and not a queue. With 100,000 approved exceptions both forms take seconds, and the cost has two parts: in the plan printed for the count of the approved with the first hub (the new form), 0.94 of its 4.3 seconds are the candidates, which scan the approved exceptions and evaluate the policy of the exceptions for each (100,000 times in that plan), and the rest is the check of each of the 50,040 candidate requests. Over the twelve pairs measured (the three choices of hub, the two scopes, the count and the list), the count of the approved took 1.1 to 6.2 seconds with the old form and 1.1 to 6.2 with the new, and the list 1.3 to 4.9 seconds with the old and 0.9 to 3.7 with the new (the new form was faster or equal in ten of the twelve pairs; the two slower are the count with no hub for a session of one hub, 4.5 to 6.2 seconds, and the count for the second hub for the same session, 1.11 to 1.13 seconds). That is a scale limit of a filter on a history that grows without bound, recorded here and not introduced by this task; the queue summary does not count it.

### 14.3 The Rider's list of requests

**Resolved by task A3.1 (the audit follow-up, the Product Owner's decision of 7 November 2026): option (iii) below is what was built.** `listPickupRequests` and `getPickupRequest` no longer name the Rider's credential or surface (contract 5.99.0; `surfaces/rider-android.md`: "Not a rider act"), so a Rider's bearer is `SESSION_INVALID` and the service refuses a Rider itself as well; the fallback of the afternoon (ignore `hub_id` for a Rider) and its measurements below are the history that led there and no longer describe the code.

*Task F, the Product Owner's rule of 7 November 2026, afternoon: if the Rider's own predicate cannot lead the statement, `hub_id` is ignored for Rider sessions, and the reason is recorded here.*

**What a Rider is shown.** No request, with or without a hub: the registry gives a Rider no `PickupRequest` (the Rider's columns of the row in [data-scope-registry.md](../../contracts/data-scope-registry.md) section 4 are `DENY`), and the table's read policy has an arm for staff and one for a Vendor and none for a Rider under the key of the list. The list is served to a Rider because the Rider bundle holds `pickup.read` (for its stops), and it answers the empty page. A Rider therefore has **no column on a request that its policy tests**, so there is no predicate of its own on `pickup_request` to name in the statement, as there is on a run (`assigned_rider_id`) or for a Vendor (`vendor_organization_id`).

**Measured** on 7 November 2026 on the machine and server of 14.1 and 14.2 (`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)`, the best of four, the runtime role and the real context of a Rider under `pickup.read`): **220,000 requests** (200,000 in the second hub, 20,000 in the first), 23,000 runs, and a Rider with **40 dispatched runs and 400 stops** on 400 of the first hub's requests; milliseconds, for the Rider's list of requests, first page, with the hub named in the column heading:

| Statement | no hub | the Rider's own hub (20,000) | the second hub (200,000) | a hub that does not exist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A.** the hub applied, and no predicate of the Rider's (the statement before task A1) | 224 | 22 | 264 | 0.07 |
| **B.** the hub applied, and the one predicate a request has that is a Rider's: it is on a stop of a run assigned to the Rider | 1.9 | 2.7 | 2.4 | 0.17 |
| **C.** the hub applied, and a constant false (a Rider is shown no request) | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.03 | 0.05 |

The statement a Rider's list runs **now** (the hub judged and ignored) is the *no hub* cell of **A**: 224 milliseconds for every hub, a scan of the table with the policy evaluated for each row, which finds nothing (0.31 to 0.38 seconds end to end, `alone/hub-filter-timing.db.test.ts`). It is the same for a hub that exists and for one that does not, which is what `AC-HUBF-05` and the audit's finding A1 ask of a read.

**Can a predicate of the Rider's own lead the statement? A predicate can; none of the Rider's own is the policy's.** Statement **B** is planned from the Rider's index: the 40 runs through `pickup_manifest_rider_idx`, their ten stops each through the stop table's key, and the 400 requests by primary key, each removed by the policy (1.9 milliseconds, against 224). It is not added, for three reasons. **(a) No policy tests it.** The policy shows a Rider no request, so the predicate changes no answer, only the plan, and it would write into the service a scope ("a Rider's requests are those on its runs") that the registry denies. Row-level security still decides, so it could not widen; but a hint that states what the registry says the Rider may not have is a second, wrong statement of scope, and this repository keeps one owner for each fact. **(b) It does not end the difference.** A hub that exists takes 2.4 milliseconds and one that does not 0.17, because the planner starts from the hub's index when the hub holds nothing: the difference is the Rider's own 400 stops and not the hub's size, the same residual a Vendor's list has (section 14.2), but it is a difference. **(c) The Product Owner's rule is about exactly this case**, and ignoring the filter has no difference to leave: the answer is the one the Rider is given without it and the time is the time without it, for every hub.

**What the choice costs, and the alternatives for the Product Owner (none built).** A Rider that asks for the list pays one scan of the table for each call (0.2 seconds at 220,000 requests, growing with the table). It is a cost the list had before any hub filter, and no Rider screen calls it. (i) **Answer a Rider's list of requests with the empty page and no statement** (statement **C**: 0.05 milliseconds for every hub, no scan): one condition in the service and its tests, as a staff session's hub outside its grant is answered, stating in the service the fact the registry states (a Rider has no request); the cleanest, and a choice about where that fact is held that this task did not take. (ii) The predicate of **B**, with reasons (a) and (b) against it. (iii) **Do not serve the list to a Rider at all** (remove `riderSession` from `listPickupRequests`): the Rider's app has no use for a list of requests, and its bundle holds `pickup.read` for its stops; a contract change.

# Glossary

Current shared terminology supplements the master and domain contracts. Use stable product and machine terms consistently; definitions belong in one owning source.

| Term| Meaning| Why it was needed| Canonical home|
|---|---|---|---|
| `Order`| The single itemized delivery unit, carrying both physical and commercial facts. "Parcel" is display language for the physical article and is not an entity, table, API resource or field prefix.| Not coined but **disambiguated**. §34.4 names the concept `Itemized Parcel / Order` with a slash, and §34.1 lists parcels and orders as if separate. `MSC-DEC-187` ruled one entity, one noun.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.7|
| `commercial_state`| The `Order` lifecycle from §36.11 — pricing, credit reservation, prepayment, closure, reversal.| §36.11 and §36.9 define two orthogonal lifecycles over one order and never name the split. Without distinct field names they collapse into one enum.| `contracts/state-machines.md` §8|
| `fulfilment_state`| The `Order` lifecycle from §36.9 — dispatch readiness through delivery, reattempt, handoff and return.| As above.| `contracts/state-machines.md` §9|
| `Evidence`| One polymorphic entity carrying the seven attributes §34.7 requires of every controlled evidence object.| §34.7 describes the attributes and insists evidence is "not unstructured incidental attachments," but names no entity. Every capture point needs the same seven facts.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §3.6|
| `HubAssignment`| An effective-dated record of a temporary hub assignment, separate from primary hub membership.| §34.9 requires temporary assignments that "must not overwrite primary membership" but names no record.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §5.8|
| `_minor` suffix| Mandatory suffix on every monetary field, denoting a minor-unit integer in Ghana pesewas.| §34.6 and `MSC-DEC-179` fix the currency and fixed-decimal representation but not the naming. The suffix makes a unit error visible at the call site rather than in production.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §3.2|
| ~~Snapshot~~| —| **Withdrawn 14 Aug 2026.** Registered on the belief that generalising `MSC-DEC-177` to all priced facts was our inference. Reading §35 showed the rule already stated generally: §35.1.5 requires historical transactions to "preserve the values, reasons, prices, provider details, and actors applicable when the transaction occurred," and §35.1.4 makes settings forward-acting by default. The term is the specification's, so it is cited, not registered.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §3.7 cites §35.1.4–5|
| Operational code| A short human-readable code for records people quote aloud, held alongside the non-guessable identifier. Never a path parameter.| §34.7 permits "human-readable operational codes" without naming the field or fixing a format. Format is `OQ-052`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §3.1|
| `AWAITING_RECIPIENT_CONFIRMATION`, `CLOSED`, `REVERSED`| Three `Order` state names.| §36.7 lists eleven required milestones in prose and §36.11 gives the commercial diagram; these three milestones had no machine name. §36.7 authorises this layer to publish exact enum names "without introducing new business policy."| `contracts/state-machines.md` §8–§9|
| `SecurityRiskHold`| A hold placed on a vendor for security or risk reasons, independent of suspension, overdue status and allowance disablement.| §35.12.8 names four conditions that "must not be collapsed into one flag" but names no record for the fourth.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| `CashHandover`| The record of one rider handing collected cash to a hub, carrying the **rider-declared total** and the **hub-confirmed total** as independent figures.| §26.3 requires the system to compare *"expected cash by parcel/payment, rider-declared handover, and hub-confirmed receipt"* and **names no record to hold the last two**. Without one the comparison it mandates cannot be performed. Same gap that produced `HubAssignment` and `Evidence`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.11|
| `MfaFactor`| The record of an enrolled second factor — owner, type, status, encrypted secret, replay metadata. **Authoritative in place of `mfa_enrolled`**, which becomes derived.| §37.2 requires MFA before privileged access and the contract carried a **boolean** as the evidence. A boolean can be set by a seed, a migration or an administrator; it asserts that a factor exists rather than being one. `MSC-DEC-262`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| `SetupGrant`| A single-use, principal-bound, purpose-bound token that lets a principal establish their **own** first credential. **Not a session** and carries no business authority.| Every first credential in the product — staff password, rider PIN, vendor secret, bootstrap password — had **no path by which its owner could establish it**, and the practical alternative is an administrator choosing someone else's permanent credential. `MSC-DEC-259`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| Authentication readiness| **Derived**, never stored: `status = ACTIVE`, a credential established, and — for a privileged bundle — an `ACTIVE` `MfaFactor`.| The programme conflated *being assigned authority* with *being able to authenticate with it*, which made privileged onboarding circular. No mutable flag is introduced, because a flag is a second source of truth that can disagree with the records it summarises — the failure `mfa_enrolled` demonstrated. `MSC-DEC-259`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| Device credential| A server-generated high-entropy secret identifying a **registered vendor browser**, stored hashed and delivered in a long-lived `HttpOnly` cookie.| §11.2 makes the vendor's registered device half the credential, and the contract represented it as a **typed string** that proves nothing. Rider-style hardware keys do not fit a deliberately **shared** account. `MSC-DEC-265`.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| Privileged bundle| The Senior Ops or Platform Admin bundle — the two §11.3 bundles whose holders **must complete MFA before access** (§11.2, §37.2). Used as the trigger for a stricter control rather than as an authority level.| The specification says "privileged access", "privileged actions" and "privileged approval" and **never names the set of bundles that are privileged**. `domain-model.md` §6.8 has depended on the term since `SLICE-000` — *"a privileged bundle may not be assigned to an identity without `mfa_enrolled`"* — and it was **never registered**. `MSC-DEC-248` makes it load-bearing: it decides which grants need a Platform Admin approver. **Not a rank.** §11.6: a permission is binary and "anything resembling a privilege level belongs in the scope attached to the grant, not the key."| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.8|
| Security context| The per-transaction envelope carrying `principal_type`, `principal_id`, `session_id`, `surface`, `hub_scope_mode`, `authorized_hub_ids`, `vendor_organization_id`, `rider_id` and `correlation_id` into PostgreSQL as **transaction-local** settings. **Discarded at commit or rollback.**| §37.3 and §11.6 fix *which* scope an actor holds and name no mechanism for delivering it to the database. On a pooled connection the mechanism is the whole control: a session-wide setting survives the request and the next caller inherits it. `MSC-DEC-275`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.1|
| Hub scope mode — `SET` / `ALL`| Whether an actor is bounded to a defined set of hubs or holds explicit all-hub authority. **`ALL` is never inferred** — not from an empty hub list, a missing context, a client request, or a bundle name.| §37.3 permits both an authorised **set** and all-hub access and never names the distinction, so a single `hub_id` would have been the natural implementation and cannot express either end of it. **An empty set means no rows and must never be read as all hubs.** `MSC-DEC-275`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.2|
| Scope class| The declared security boundary of a persistent table: `PLATFORM`, `HUB`, `VENDOR`, `RIDER_ASSIGNMENT`, `AUDIT` or `SYSTEM`.| [domain-model.md](../contracts/domain-model.md) §4 names two scoping **axes** for sixteen entity groups. A row-level-security policy needs a per-table class, the field it reads, the parent it derives from, and whether that field may change — none of which §4 was written to carry. `MSC-DEC-277`.| [data-scope-registry.md](../contracts/data-scope-registry.md) §2|
| Derived scope metadata| A `responsible_hub_id` or `vendor_organization_id` copied onto an operational child at creation, **immutable afterwards** and held to its parent by a composite foreign key.| A policy cannot follow a multi-hop join, and §5.6's polymorphic `Evidence` owner *“cannot be constrained by foreign key”* — a modelling cost that becomes a security property under RLS. **Immutability is what stops the copy becoming a second, editable truth.** `MSC-DEC-278`, `MSC-DEC-282`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.5|
| Integrity checkpoint| A periodic, KMS-signed manifest over a batch of `AuditEvent` records, **referencing the previous checkpoint's hash**, written to immutable storage.| §38.1 requires history that *“cannot be silently rewritten by normal users”* and names no mechanism. Append-only enforced by table permissions constrains the **application** and not a credential holder — and **the chain is what detects deletion**, which per-event hashing cannot. **Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof.** `MSC-DEC-283`.| [audit.md](../contracts/audit.md) §2.1|
| Backup plane| Snapshots, WAL/PITR archives and object versions, held under access control **separate from the application** and unreachable with application credentials.| §40.4 sets recovery targets and says nothing about who may reach the backups. Encryption at rest defends the medium and not a credential that can delete the archive — **and `DEPLOYMENT_AND_ENVIRONMENTS.md` §6 makes that archive the real recovery position.** `MSC-DEC-284`.| [DEPLOYMENT_AND_ENVIRONMENTS.md](../architecture/DEPLOYMENT_AND_ENVIRONMENTS.md) §13.2|
| Principal-specific RLS| Row-level security evaluated **by authenticated principal type** — Staff, Vendor, Rider or SYSTEM — with mutually exclusive branches, rather than a Hub predicate with other actors layered on top.| §37.3 permits three authentication models and §34.1 gives the vendor its own records; **neither names how the two compose**, and the natural composition is wrong. A Vendor holds no `authorized_hub_ids`, so a Hub-first predicate leaves *see nothing* or *treat empty as permissive*. `MSC-DEC-286`.| [data-scope-registry.md](../contracts/data-scope-registry.md) §1|
| Outbox security envelope| The immutable, **server-derived** scope record written inside the same business transaction as the mutation it describes, and read by a worker **before** it establishes any database context.| §39.5 names the outbox pattern for delivery reliability and says nothing about authority. **A payload that establishes the authority under which it is then validated validates itself**, which is what made R0's re-check circular. `MSC-DEC-287`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.7|
| Principal-scoped idempotency| Replay identity keyed on `principal_type + principal_id + operation_id + idempotency_key`, with the payload hash evaluated inside it.| §36.1 requires idempotent replay and names no ownership for the key. **A globally-keyed record does not collide — it replays one caller's stored result to another**, turning a retry-safety mechanism into a disclosure channel. `MSC-DEC-288`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.16|
| Deployment-elevated migration identity| A database identity holding the DDL, ownership and policy authority a migration genuinely requires, **bounded by the deployment window** rather than by a claim that it is unprivileged.| §42.6 requires reviewed migrations and names no identity. **An identity that transfers table ownership is privileged**, and describing it otherwise does not constrain it — it stops anyone constraining it deliberately. `MSC-DEC-289`.| [SECURITY_DESIGN.md](../architecture/SECURITY_DESIGN.md) §14.17a|
| Block reach — `HUB_LOCAL` / `PLATFORM_WIDE`| The explicit scope discriminator on an `AdHocSenderBlock`, deciding whether a block binds one hub or every hub.| §29.6 supplies the grounds and `MSC-DEC-258` the authority; **neither states the reach**, which `OQ-104` carried. **A nullable hub meaning *everywhere* was rejected**: a defect that fails to populate it would silently escalate a hub block into a platform-wide ban. `MSC-DEC-296`.| [domain-model.md](../contracts/domain-model.md) §6.9|
| `count_variance` values `MATCH` / `SHORT` / `OVER`| The comparison **outcome** of a blind count, held separately from the numeric difference.| §35.5.4 refers to a variance of `MATCH` and requires condition and dispute findings to be recordable at that value. A bare integer cannot express "compared, and equal" as a distinct state.| `contracts/domain-model.md` §6.6|

## Rule for adding a term

1. Confirm the Master Specification does not already define it — check §51 **and** §34's concept tables, and the domain vocabulary in the section you are working from.
2. If it exists there, cite the section. Do not register it.
3. If it does not, add a row here naming the term, its meaning, why the specification's vocabulary was insufficient, and which document owns it.
4. Machine-stable terms may never be changed for display convenience. Display labels are `OQ-037` and are separated from machine codes by §36.13.
